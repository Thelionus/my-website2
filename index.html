Yves Daoust

Electroacoustic Music Composer
Born April 10, 1946 · Longueuil, Quebec, Canada

About

Yves Daoust is recognized as one of Canada's pioneers in electroacoustic music, distinguished as a composer, producer, and pedagogue. His remarkable career has spanned over five decades, during which he has profoundly influenced the landscape of contemporary electroacoustic composition in Canada and beyond.

Early Musical Journey

Daoust began his musical studies at the age of seven, studying piano with Alice Vigeant. His fascination with electroacoustic music emerged early—at sixteen, he produced his first experiments when he "prepared" the family piano to create the soundtrack for a friend's 8mm experimental film. At nineteen, he completed his first electronic work: an hour of music for a gestural theater piece that premiered in Berlin, Germany, at an international amateur theater festival.

Education & Training

  • Studied piano with Irving Heller
  • Analysis and composition with Gilles Tremblay at the Conservatoire de musique du Québec à Montréal (1966-71)
  • Music for films with Maurice Blackburn and Norman McLaren at the National Film Board (1970-1)
  • Electroacoustic composition in France with Alain Savouret, Gilbert Amy, and the Groupe de musique expérimentale de Bourges

Career Highlights

Professional Positions

Upon returning to Canada, Daoust worked in the sound studio of the National Film Board (1976-79). In 1978, he joined the staff of the Conservatoire de musique du Québec, teaching in both Montreal and Quebec City. He taught solfège and analysis, and beginning in 1981, electroacoustic composition. He directed the electroacoustic composition programme at the Conservatoire de musique et d'Art dramatique du Québec from 1980 until his retirement in 2011.

Founding Contributions

In 1978, Daoust was instrumental in founding and developing the Association pour la création et la recherche électroacoustiques du Québec (ACREQ), the first organization in Canada devoted to the promotion and presentation of electroacoustic music.

Musical Aesthetic

Daoust describes himself as "figurative," preferring natural sounds, sound archives, and musical quotations to aid his visual approach to composition.

Influences

His artistic vision has been shaped by diverse sources:

Film Scores John Cage Iannis Xenakis Mauricio Kagel Luc Ferrari Alain Savouret Karlheinz Stockhausen Ludwig van Beethoven Robert Schumann René Magritte

Recognition

Prix Serge-Garant (2009)

In recognition of his lifetime achievements and contributions to electroacoustic music, Yves Daoust received the prestigious Prix Serge-Garant from the Fondation Émile-Nelligan in 2009.

Legacy

Through his compositions, teaching, and advocacy, Yves Daoust has played a pivotal role in establishing electroacoustic music as a vital art form in Canada. His work continues to inspire new generations of composers exploring the intersection of technology, sound, and artistic expression.
